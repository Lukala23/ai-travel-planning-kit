# Air Routing, Fare Comparison, and Booking Strategy

Read only when the current request needs an air chain designed, compared, audited, or given a tracking protocol, or when an unbooked flight materially blocks the current route or cost. “A flight is not booked yet” in background context is not enough. This module also owns domestic flights within a destination.

This module owns routing, fares, comfort, connection risk, and booking strategy. `entry-and-transit.md` owns entry/transit legality; `public-transit.md` owns rail, ferry, public airport access, and local transport.

## 1. Fix the comparison basis

Extract what is relevant without re-asking settled facts:

- actual origin city/airport range, first and last trip nodes, final return point, and any intermediate air sectors; no home address;
- exact dates, flexibility, travelers, and fare-relevant age categories;
- cabin, checked/cabin baggage, seating, and through-check needs;
- acceptable stops, door-to-door duration, red-eye/overnight, airport change, self-transfer, and separate-ticket risk;
- willingness to position by surface transport or domestic air to another gateway and add a hotel;
- budget basis, change/refund flexibility, and must-arrive time; loyalty or miles only when relevant.

If dates or travelers are unresolved, compare route structures and search methods only. Never label an illustrative fare as bookable.

## 2. Start from the actual origin and return point

Compare the complete outbound and return chain first: nonstop where available, protected connections, and open-jaw or multi-city structures when the itinerary benefits.

Add another gateway only when current routing and fare evidence shows a material benefit. Include:

- positioning air/rail/coach, station-airport transfer, lodging, and materially induced meals;
- baggage on every ticket, reclaim/recheck, check-in, security, and airport changes;
- through-ticket protection and responsibility after a miss—one booking reference or marketplace order does not by itself prove protection;
- delay exposure, red-eye, long wait, full elapsed time, and effort cost.

Call a positioning option better only after complete cost, time, and risk still win. Present separate tickets as a distinct risk class.

## 3. Domestic flights within the destination

Treat an internal flight as an intercity candidate for long distances, islands, or weak surface links, but compare door-to-door rather than airborne time:

- use this module for flight, fare, baggage, fare rules, and connection risk; use `public-transit.md` for rail, coach, ferry, and airport access;
- check the operating/ticketing airline, exact airports, and local or regional sales channels that the traveler can actually use;
- include tax, low-cost extras, airport access, check-in/security, frequency, the next workable option after cancellation, and any induced hotel;
- for a same-day international connection, verify protection, entry, baggage reclaim/recheck, terminal transfer, and a practical self-transfer buffer;
- integrate arrival and departure with that day's lodging, activities, and airport access.

## 4. Search the traveler's real sales market

Use a channel mix appropriate to the traveler's origin, payment access, language, and consumer protection:

1. airline-direct or operating-carrier channels for inventory, fare brand, baggage, changes/refunds, and connection responsibility;
2. reputable local or regional travel agencies/marketplaces the traveler can buy from and obtain support from;
3. global metasearch for flexible dates, nearby airports, route discovery, and price context.

Use low-fare calendars, price signals, or alerts only when accessible and their basis is clear. Reprice finalists with identical travelers, dates, cabin, baggage, and settlement currency, ideally to the last step before confirmation. Identify member, coupon, app-only, subsidy, card, or residency conditions. No single channel proves complete market coverage.

If a logged-in or highly dynamic result is inaccessible, do not invent a fare. Provide exact search inputs and fields to record, then compare redacted user quotes or screenshots on one basis.

## 5. Normalize complete cost, time, and responsibility

For each finalist include only relevant fields:

- fare with taxes, baggage, seat, payment/agency fee, and required document cost;
- positioning, airport change/access, hotel, and induced meals;
- complete dates, time zones, terminals, overnights, and door-to-door elapsed time;
- operating carrier, fare brand, and cabin; aircraft/seat as changeable facts only;
- connection protection, ticketing/operating responsibility, through baggage, self-transfer, recheck, and practical buffer;
- refund, change, no-show, disruption protection, after-sales responsibility, and sales channel.

The lowest sticker fare is not automatically best. Balance complete cost, total duration, sleep loss, timing, baggage, protection, and usable support, and integrate the flights into the first and last trip days.

## 6. Booking timing and tracking protocol

There is no universal “best number of days before departure.” Use evidence relevant to the exact route, dates, and sales market: current normalized fare, accessible calendar/trend signal, comparable snapshots, holidays/events, date rigidity, alternatives, and change/refund cost. Statistics from another market are context only when their sample is stated.

Return `Buy now / Set an alert and observe / Wait for trip conditions / Cannot yet assess`, explaining the fare-versus-trip-risk tradeoff.

If observing, give a manual protocol: exact query, channels, current complete fare and timestamp, target range, buy trigger, latest decision date, and action if price, preferred itinerary, or terms deteriorate. Say when route-level history is insufficient for a credible trend claim.

Do not claim continuous monitoring or proactive notification unless the host actually provides scheduling and persistent storage and the user explicitly authorizes it. Reprice at checkout after any alert.

## 7. Delivery and action boundary

Keep only materially distinct finalists, normally no more than `Balanced / Lowest complete cost / More comfortable or lower risk`; do not manufacture options.

| Option | Complete air chain | Complete price/time | Baggage/fare brand | Connection/protection | Comfort | Change/refund/support | Induced cost/risk | Source/time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then state the choice, booking strategy, and next action. Without explicit authorization, do not log in, redeem points, enter traveler documents, book, or pay. Never expose order, identity, phone, loyalty, or payment data in screenshots or output.
