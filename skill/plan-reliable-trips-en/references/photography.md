# Mobile-First Photography Rules

Photography is one input to route quality, not the only fixed objective. Scan the entire executable route for genuine visual and social-sharing value, then recommend photographs that an ordinary traveler can reproduce with a phone or pocket camera through position, timing, composition, and light rather than professional equipment.

## 1. Evaluation standard

Do not equate popularity with photographic value. Consider:

- uniqueness and connection to the place;
- readable subject, foreground/background relationship, depth, scale, and human activity;
- light direction, shadow, weather, season, crowd pattern, and temporary obstruction;
- whether the public position and result are realistically reproducible;
- time, queue, detour, access, safety, legal, cultural, and opportunity cost;
- value as a travel memory and value as a strong social image, which may differ.

A place with ordinary visual drama but high travel meaning still deserves a good record-photo method. A viral angle that requires a drone, restricted roof, private land, dangerous roadside position, extreme telephoto compression, rare season, or heavy edit must be identified rather than presented as a normal result.

## 2. Route-wide grading

Grade opportunities as:

- `Must shoot`: major photographic or social-posting value with acceptable total cost;
- `Worth shooting`: useful composition or memory value and naturally compatible with the route;
- `Record only`: meaningful place, but no reason to spend extra time chasing a polished frame;
- `Not worth a photo detour`: high time, access, crowd, or safety cost relative to the obtainable image.

The grade affects detail, not whether the attraction stays in the trip. A core cultural site may remain essential even when it is only `Record only`.

## 3. Priority photo card

For every `Must shoot` and important `Worth shooting` point, provide:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Public position | Copyable place/entrance/viewpoint name and precise public standing area; map link when reliable |
| Subject and direction | What to photograph, camera direction, and useful foreground/background |
| Composition | Framing, height, vertical/horizontal choice, leading lines, symmetry/asymmetry, scale, and people placement |
| Light and timing | Useful time window, sun direction, weather/season condition, and crowd effect |
| Phone method | Practical lens choice, exposure/focus, panorama/night/portrait mode only when useful; no assumed pro gear |
| Cost | Extra start time, wait, detour, ticket, reservation, or sacrificed experience |
| Restrictions | Tripod, flash, commercial photography, drone, privacy, worship, security, or wildlife rules |
| Fallback | A usable alternate angle or record-photo method for rain, crowd, closure, or bad light |
| References | One to three real source images/pages and the specific feature to learn from each |

For ordinary famous attractions, still provide concise advice on how to avoid the default cluttered snapshot: best public side, height, edge framing, foreground, human scale, and a practical crowd-handling method.

## 4. Reference-image standard

Priority points should include one to three real, sourced visual references. If words cannot explain a useful composition at an ordinary famous place, include at least one there too.

For each reference:

- show an actual preview when lawful and technically possible; otherwise link to the original page;
- identify original source, photographer/organisation when available, and verification date;
- explain what to learn: position, direction, foreground, scale, light, colour, or human placement;
- flag drone, long lens, stitched panorama, heavy edit, rare weather, special event, private/non-public position, or outdated access;
- do not use an unattributed repost as the only evidence for a position.

References are for planning and study, not for copying protected work or trespassing to reproduce it. Respect licence, attribution, privacy, and platform embedding rules.

## 5. Integrating photography into the route

- Use current sunrise, sunset, twilight, solar direction, opening, transport, and weather for the actual date and coordinates.
- Put a worthwhile, safe early start into the main route when the experience justifies it; the user accepts early starts.
- If a shot requires a long wait, major detour, special transport, expensive room, sacrificed core experience, or materially higher risk, present it as a separate `Photography upgrade`, with cost and what it displaces.
- Do not make every stop occur at golden hour. Allocate the scarce light window to the few places that gain most from it.
- Identify good en-route street, transit, landscape, food, market, and accommodation frames rather than limiting photography to official attractions.
- For weather-dependent views, give a normal-condition plan and a bad-weather alternative instead of promising a result.

## 6. Phone and pocket-camera defaults

Assume a phone and, at most, a compact device such as an Osmo Pocket unless the current trip says otherwise. Do not make equipment a required questionnaire field.

Prioritise:

- clean lens, stable stance, level horizon, intentional subject, and edge control;
- moving a few metres, changing height, waiting briefly for separation, or using a foreground before digital zoom;
- exposure protection around bright sky, window, snow, or stage light;
- a small number of repeatable instructions instead of technical settings that the device may not support.

Mention a tripod, filter, remote, drone, telephoto, or specialised protection only when it materially changes this specific opportunity and remains legal and practical.

## 7. Safety, legality, and ethics

Never recommend standing in traffic, crossing barriers, occupying railways, climbing unstable edges, interfering with worship, photographing restricted security areas, harassing wildlife, or using a drone illegally. Check museum, performance, religious, private-property, portrait, child, tripod, flash, and commercial-use rules when relevant.

If a position becomes unsafe or disruptive under crowd, tide, ice, darkness, heat, or traffic conditions, give a safer alternative. A photograph never overrides the trip's safety, law, hard booking, or safe-return requirements.
