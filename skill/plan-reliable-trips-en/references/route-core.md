# General route-design rules

Read for a daily/timed route or route comparison.

## 1. Complete time model

Calculate each segment:

`total = activity + movement + wait/transfer + queue/security + meal/rest + wayfinding/storage + buffer`

- Map time is ideal, not a promise. Give separate margin to long links and important bookings.
- Use consequence-based buffer, not one whole-day percentage. Ordinary replaceable nodes need reasonable flexibility; performances, timed guides, non-refundable bookings, flights/intercity, last service, return day, and weather-sensitive activities need enough safety margin.
- Default ordinary sightseeing to balanced coverage; switch to holiday or high coverage only from current intent.
- Describe proposed intensity through start/finish, core blocks, walking/standing, earliest/latest hard booking, and consecutive demanding days. Ask only when it changes core route or expensive bookings.
- Deduct airport/station, luggage, check-in/out, and intercity cost on arrival/departure days.
- Give the last admission/closure of the last hard-closing node.
- Route from the final node back to accommodation or transport; never end naturally at an attraction.
- Treat confirmed bookings as anchors. Separate hard anchors, core experiences, movable nodes, and spare-capacity additions; cut low priority before transport, food, rest, and safe return.
- First reserve a realistic meal window and convenient area. A high-value or reservation meal may anchor; read `dining.md` through the SKILL router only when an actual food choice needs research.
- Perform only a one-line photography-value scan across ordinary sights. Read `photography.md` through the SKILL router only when a high-value opportunity changes route tradeoffs, photography is explicit, or formal delivery needs reference images.
- Raise priority when high photo and independently judged social-sharing value coexist at manageable cost; otherwise compare normal and photography-upgrade plans.
- When a museum is a route core, load `museum-visits.md` through the SKILL router for security, storage, interpretation, internal movement, fatigue, and shortened routes; do not model a major museum as a generic one-hour sight.

## 2. Season and near-term weather

- At skeleton stage, check daylight, typical rain/heat/cold/wind/fog, flood/snow/typhoon season, sea state, vegetation, and seasonal closure. Climate is not a trip forecast.
- In a reliable window, update with official or high-quality local forecasts and warnings. Use mountain/coast/desert/water/trail conditions rather than city-centre weather.
- Use risk-appropriate rechecks: city activities may use T-7/T-48; high-risk or volatile activities may need prior-evening/same-day authority updates.
- State affected activities and triggers for retime, day swap, indoor replacement, shortening, or cancellation.
- Reorder indoor/outdoor, photography, and transport before cutting. Never execute against official closure or danger limits.

## 3. Route-node standard

Every node includes arrival window, dwell, travel mode, booking level, time limit, experience focus, and exit.

- `Booking required`: normally impossible without a slot/ticket.
- `Strongly advise`: walk-up may exist but sell-out/queue risk is high.
- `Walk-up possible`: usually does not structure the day.
- `Uncertain`: current evidence is insufficient.

When a service is not confirmed, give an operating window and latest choice, not an invented departure minute.

## 4. City walks and day trips

- Use a clear theme or spatial axis and avoid zig-zagging.
- Anchor on fixed opening and high-value experiences, then add flexible nodes.
- State order, walking, ascent, intensity, and main trade-off.
- Give real meal windows; use an exact venue for local value or booking need, otherwise a screened zone and primary/backups.
- Add only fallbacks for real current energy, season/weather, closure, or delay risks. Do not make a late-start plan by default. Each fallback needs trigger, first cut, replacement, and booking/return effect.

## 5. Multi-day trips

- Organise each day by zone, theme, or corridor; cross zones only for clear gain.
- Avoid consecutive high-intensity stacking and plan recovery for the travelers.
- Build route and candidate accommodation areas, then actively search exact properties and recompute daily routes, nearby walking, and food. Continue adjusting when they conflict.
- Count packing, checkout, storage, movement, check-in wait, and luggage for property moves. Reduce no-value moves but use multiple bases when a special stay's view, architecture, hot spring, local life, photography, or route benefit justifies it.
- Mark intensity, movement, hard bookings, and weather-swap conditions.
- Use real usable time for arrival/departure; “half day” is not a fixed duration.

## 6. Costs, meals, and fallbacks

- Split ticket, transport, accommodation, meal, activity, and reserve; label per person/room/vehicle and taxes.
- Give verification date and range for dynamic prices; avoid false total precision.
- Research food whenever a route crosses a meal. Use exact venues for unique value, reservation, or route improvement; otherwise give a credible area and backups.
- Give a non-conflicting alternative for every hard booking or weather-sensitive day.
- Name each day's first cut or shortening. For a failed critical connection, give the next action, not only an unverified backup sight.
