# Souvenirs and Local Purchases

Read only when the user wants a memento/gift or a purchase has become an explicit route stop. Passing a museum, cultural site, market, or craft area warrants only a light value scan, not an automatic module load or shopping plan. This remains a light planning module, not a shopping itinerary. The default preference is for items connected to the place, interesting, portable, and reasonably priced; handmade or uncommon qualities are bonuses, not gates.

## 1. What to recommend

Separate two needs:

- **Personal memento**: a small object with design, museum, craft, route, or place memory value;
- **Gifts for friends**: easy to divide, carry, explain, and give without creating a high budget burden.

Reasonable categories include magnets, postcards, stationery, museum or cultural-site derivatives, small prints, bookmarks, pins, practical design goods, small textiles, local craft, compliant packaged food, tea/coffee/spice products, or other destination-specific items. Do not assume the traveler wants valuable jewellery, antiques, luxury goods, or a purchase at every attraction.

## 2. Price tiers

Use local market conditions rather than one universal currency threshold:

- `Low-cost, many recipients`: small gifts that can be bought in multiples;
- `Mid-range, better quality`: a few objects with better material, design, taste, or provenance;
- `A few special pieces`: only when the place offers something genuinely distinctive and the user's current budget supports it.

Give local-currency prices and a useful home-currency conversion with exchange date. State whether the unit is per piece, set, weight, or package. Use a range when bargaining, material, artist, size, or season changes the price.

## 3. Where to buy

Compare relevant channels instead of declaring one universally cheapest:

- museum or heritage-site shop;
- maker studio, cooperative, craft centre, or certified local shop;
- traditional market or neighbourhood market;
- design shop, bookshop, stationery shop, or gallery shop;
- supermarket or specialty food shop;
- tourist street, hotel, station, and airport shop.

Assess local distinctiveness, quality, price transparency, authenticity claim, return/exchange, packaging, payment, route convenience, opening, and pressure to buy. Airport or attraction shops may be convenient but are not automatically best; markets may be cheaper but are not automatically local or authentic.

## 4. Recommendation card

Keep each item concise:

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Item | Local/English name and what makes it relevant |
| Recipient | Self, many friends, or a special gift |
| Price | Local range, unit, and useful conversion |
| Where | Specific shop/area/channel and why it fits |
| Route fit | Which day/node and whether it creates a detour |
| Carrying | Size, weight, fragility, shelf life, liquid, smell, temperature, or packaging |
| Legality | Customs, quarantine, protected material, food, antique/cultural-property, or airline restrictions |
| Status | Verified, representative estimate, or recheck before purchase |

Prioritise a small shortlist. Avoid long generic shopping catalogues.

## 5. Claims and risk

- Verify provenance only when the price is material or the seller claims handmade, limited, antique, protected-origin, precious material, or named-maker status.
- Do not use “traditional,” “local,” or “handmade” solely because a tourist shop says so.
- For food, plants, seeds, animal products, wood, coral, shells, medicines, alcohol, blades, batteries, liquids, antiques, and cultural objects, check destination export, arrival-country import, and carrier rules.
- Flag aggressive sales, commission stops, inflated starting prices, counterfeit brands, fake certificates, and shipping/refund risks when credible evidence shows they affect the proposed place.
- Do not make a souvenir stop displace a core experience unless the user explicitly values shopping.
