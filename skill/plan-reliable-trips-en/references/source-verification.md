# Research and verification rules

## 1. Identify dynamic facts

Research these in the current task:

- operating days, hours, last admission, seasonal or temporary closure;
- tickets, reservations, release times, and pass coverage;
- current museum exhibitions, closed galleries, objects on display, internal route, interpretation, external-guide policy, and price;
- bus, rail, ferry, shuttle services and suspensions;
- visa, entry, roads, parking, restrictions, drones, and protected-area rules;
- trail opening, works, rockfall, fire, avalanche, crossings, tide, permits, and availability/version of route and waypoints;
- forecast, air quality, sunrise/sunset, tide, snow, and water level;
- photo-point access, restrictions, obstruction/works, crowd conditions, sun direction, and source/conditions of reference images;
- accommodation legality, room inventory, total price, tax, deposit, cancellation, and check-in;
- restaurant/market status, address, menu, price, booking, closed days, food-safety evidence, tax, mandatory service, cover/card fee, and tipping custom;
- tours, vehicles, inventory, prices, and exchange rate;
- wallet/card acceptance, ATM/exchange cost, roaming/SIM/eSIM, device compatibility, and coverage;
- taxi regulation/fixed fare, app coverage/estimate, transfer/charter/unusual-transport quote, pickup point, licence, equipment, animal welfare, and scam pattern;
- official border fees and repeatedly reported unofficial demands in official advisories and credible recent sources;
- every claim using “current,” “latest,” “now,” “this year,” or a future operating date.

Stable history and architecture still need credible support. Preserve disagreements.

## 2. Source hierarchy

Prefer direct pages in this order:

1. government, parks/protected areas, attraction/museum, rail/transit, road, meteorological, and emergency authorities;
2. tourism boards, authoritative heritage bodies, official maps and official PDF/GPX;
3. major maps, credible publications, and professional outdoor databases for location, experience, and cross-checking;
4. recent visitors for queues, works, wayfinding, and field experience—not opening, safety, or law alone.

Open the supporting page, not a search-result page, aggregator, or undated snippet. Search non-English destinations in local language and official English names.

### Domain-specific use

- **Accommodation and dining**: official channels prove room/facility/menu/policy; booking platforms prove current inventory, comparable total, and channel terms; maps, local platforms, and recent customers reveal experience and repeated problems. No one score proves “best” or “locals' favourite.”
- **Hotel/restaurant charges**: use tax/local government, consumer authority, current terms/menu, and booking confirmation. A customer bill only discovers a charge; it cannot prove tax or national custom by itself. Separate statutory tax, mandatory surcharge, and voluntary tip.
- **Borders**: use immigration, customs, embassies/consulates, and competent authorities for official fees. Cross-check unofficial-demand risk with official advisories and several credible, recent, independent sources. Keep airport commercial fast services separate from immigration/customs power.
- **Museums**: use current official opening/ticket/exhibition pages, collection databases, gallery maps, interpretation, and guide policies. Database holdings are not automatically on display. Use cultural authorities, associations, universities/research, catalogues, and credible specialist sources for value; use visitors for queue, wayfinding, and actual language availability. Verify guide price from the museum, official/licensed directory, lawful operator, or exact written quote.
- **Photography**: prefer official destination/cultural sources, traceable creators, and Wikimedia Commons; use map/social imagery to cross-check current ordinary reproducibility. Give original source page, creator/organisation where available, and verification date. Detect drone, telephoto, heavy edit, rare season, closure, and non-public position.
- **Taxis/charters/unusual transport**: prefer regulator, airport/attraction fixed fare, current route estimate, and licensed written quote. Visitor reports reveal scam patterns, not standard price. Use regulation and current field evidence for equipment and animal welfare. If no route/time price can be verified, explain how to obtain comparable quotes rather than inventing a range.

## 3. Verification strength

- Safety, legality, and expensive non-refundable decisions: at least two independent sources, including one official source.
- Opening, tickets, booking, and service times: at least one direct official source; add a second and flag conflict when official information is vague or stale.
- Map time and distance: estimates; cross-check critical links by another method.
- User-generated content: subjective experience or issue discovery only.

Copied text across sites is not independent evidence.

## 4. Dates and weather

- Convert relative dates to complete date and weekday and show the interpretation; preserve ambiguity when needed.
- Inside a useful forecast window, use official/local high-quality forecasts with update time.
- Outside it, provide climate/seasonal risk labelled `Not a forecast for the trip`, with T-7/T-48 or appropriate rechecks.
- Use local mountain, coast, gorge, or marine forecasts; a city forecast cannot substitute.
- Write full dates and weekdays so “tomorrow” and “weekend” do not expire inside the document.

## 5. Resolve conflicts

1. Compare publisher, applicable date, update, time zone, and audience.
2. Prefer the newest rule closest to the actual operator or competent authority.
3. If still unresolved, show both versions and consequences; do not decide by guess.
4. Mark conflicts that could cause denied access, stranding, or danger for mandatory human confirmation.

Silence on a page does not prove no reservation, no risk, or open passage.

## 6. Citation and status

Place a clickable direct source beside the sentence or cell it supports and give a verification date for critical dynamic facts.

Use plain `Information status` labels:

- `Verified`: current official or strong direct evidence supports it;
- `Recheck before departure`: usable now but changeable;
- `Conditional`: depends on weather, permit, transport, or unresolved user input;
- `Uncertain`: conflicting, stale, or inaccessible sources;
- `Planning judgment`: inferred recommendation, not source wording.

These labels describe evidence and change risk, not A/B/C/D travel value.

Never invent a URL, citation, fare, coordinate, schedule, or “official recommendation.” State when browsing or access is limited.
