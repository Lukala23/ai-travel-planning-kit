# Local food research and route integration

Read when the user wants local-food research, restaurants/markets/street shops, or a meal becomes a route anchor or material choice. A route crossing a main meal still reserves a realistic window and convenient area, but that alone does not trigger the full dining module.

## 1. Decide what to eat before where

- Build candidates for representative dishes, staples, breakfast, snacks, sweets, drinks, markets, seasonal and regional food.
- Separate everyday local food, festival/seasonal food, culturally valuable but poor-fit items, and tourist-oriented products.
- Screen by dietary limits, budget, street-food acceptance, queue/booking tolerance, schedule, and travelers.
- Avoid repeated dishes added for quantity; let important meals cover different aspects.

## 2. Use multiple source types

Combine what exists locally:

- official venue/market/chef pages, menus, booking, and current hours;
- recent Google Maps text, ordinary photos, menus, and Q&A;
- local-language map, review, booking, or delivery platforms used by residents;
- local press, long-running food writers, professional criticism, cuisine research, and credible guides;
- Michelin, Bib Gourmand, Gault&Millau, or local awards only where informative; an award is not automatically the best fit;
- lifestyle, short-video, blog, and social sources for candidate discovery and recent field clues only.

Google Maps is useful for cross-checking but visitor mix, marketing, rating manipulation, and language bias prevent it from deciding alone.

## 3. Support “local clientele” carefully

Do not call something a local favourite from promotional copy. Check:

- local-language review content, time distribution, and recent consistency;
- locally common dishes, seasonal ingredients, and reasonable local prices;
- residential, office, traditional-market, or established neighbourhood rather than only tourist strip;
- weekday meal traffic clues, repeat-customer descriptions, and ordinary photos;
- long-term mention across independent local sources;
- chef/address changes, decline, menu shrink, or touristification.

These support planning judgments such as `strong local-clientele evidence / mixed clientele / tourist-oriented`, not knowledge of every customer.

## 4. Screen each route venue

- `Local value`: regional dish, ingredient, technique, or setting and irreplaceability.
- `Actual quality`: recent consistency, signature versus ordinary dishes, portion, repeated negatives.
- `Price value`: per-person range; statutory tax; mandatory service, cover/bread/water, minimum, card surcharge; optional tip; cash need. Keep them separate.
- `Route fit`: real detour from previous/next/base, operating window, and fallback after a miss.
- `Wait and booking`: requirement, queue mechanism, lost-turn rule, meal time, and lateness.
- `Diet fit`: allergen, exclusion, vegetarian, halal, children, and language; retain risk when unknown.
- `Field conditions`: seating, hygiene evidence, smoking, indoor/outdoor climate, toilets, companion comfort.
- `Information status`: current menu, price, hours, address, and booking.

Street stalls, small shops, market counters, and ordinary restaurants can rank equally. Never guarantee food safety from appearance. Prefer high turnover, fresh cooking, reasonable storage, no repeated recent hygiene issue, and official hygiene evidence where available.

## 5. Filter viral noise

- Look for identical angles/signature dishes/copy, influencer packages, gifts, rebates, and undisclosed promotion.
- Compare real menu, portion, wait, service, price, and ordinary photos to detect photogenic but poor-value food.
- Retain a popular venue when dish or setting is irreplaceable, but state queue/premium and a more local alternative.
- Popularity without independent quality and local-value evidence is not a core recommendation.

## 6. Put meals into the route

- Accommodation-area evaluation includes actually open breakfast/dinner, local shops, and safe night walkability, not restaurant count.
- Connect breakfast to base/first route, lunch to morning/afternoon geography, dinner to last node/night walk/safe return.
- Count arrival, queue, ordering, and eating. A reservation or irreplaceable market may anchor; an ordinary meal should not cause a cross-city return.
- Give a same-area primary and backup for important meals.
- Do not force every meal to one venue. Use exact places for high value or booking; otherwise `area + two or three screened candidates + field selection rule`.
- When food justifies a dedicated trip, state uniqueness, detour, wait, and its trade-off against sights, lodging, and budget.

## 7. Bills, tips, and unclear charges

- Verify current tax/consumer information and venue menu/terms; customer bills discover possible items but do not prove legality or universality.
- Separate statutory tax, mandatory service, cover/bread/water, minimum spend, card fee, and voluntary tip, with amount, inclusion, collector, and whether a service fee changes normal tipping.
- Never confuse a hospitality tip with an unofficial payment to a public official.
- For an unexplained percentage/item, ask for itemised bill, name, mandatory/optional status, and menu/legal basis; compare ordering price and ask a manager to explain/correct.
- Match terminal amount/currency to the bill and avoid duplicate tip. Prioritise safety; preserve bill, menu, and payment evidence for consumer authority, tourist police, booking platform, or issuer as appropriate.

## 8. Deliverable

First state what is worth eating, then integrate it:

| Date/meal | Area and route | Primary/backup | Dishes and local value | Per person, tax/service/tip | Hours/booking/queue | Payment/diet | Why credible | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- Use a searchable local name and map/booking link; date prices, hours, and status.
- Separate official menu, local platform, Google Maps, and planning judgment.
- Replace unsupported “locals go here” with verifiable facts.
- Summarise representative-food coverage, meal anchors, field-decision meals, and whether base-area dinner and backups work.
