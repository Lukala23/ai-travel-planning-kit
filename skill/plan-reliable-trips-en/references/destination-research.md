# Destination candidate research and grading

Use for a new destination, broad candidate request, or the screening needed before route design. Research depth follows the trip; do not force a full list as a separate deliverable.

## 1. Define the boundary

State administrative area, normal visitor area, realistic day-trip region, and whether this trip includes each. Avoid double-counting aliases, sub-attractions, or independent destinations.

Search selectively for:

- representative sights, historic structures, streets, and urban spaces;
- national/specialist museums, art, archaeology/history, memorial, site, house, university, high-value small museums, temporary exhibitions, performance, and heritage;
- parks, viewpoints, coast, protected areas, and trails;
- representative food, local dining areas, markets, daily life, shopping, and night experiences;
- seasonal, reservation-only, lesser-known high-fit places;
- transport hubs, functional accommodation areas, experiential stays, and possible anchors.

“Comprehensive” means the realistic set needed for current decisions, not every map business.

Lightly scan museums for every destination and deepen the search in capitals, major cities, and historic/cultural cities. When a venue matters, apply `museum-visits.md` to actual display, curation, language access, time, and overlap; do not limit research to famous tourist museums or treat all museums as rain fallbacks.

### Screen marketing noise

Use social platforms, lists, ratings, and likes only to discover candidates. For a highly visible or high-premium product, check concentrated copy/angles, disclosure of commercial relationships, consistency with recent ordinary traveler text/original images/negative reports, dependence on drones, long-lens compression, heavy editing, closed positions, or a tiny seasonal window, and whether the real content justifies queue, ticket, detour, and extras. Check whether different platforms merely repeat one asset. Use `High marketing risk` only with observable reasons; without sufficient evidence, do not allege rating manipulation, fabrication, or fraud.

## 2. Build spatial and time structure

- Group by zone, transport corridor, or entrance.
- Calculate door-to-door cost between zones.
- Identify opening, booking, season, light, and last-service constraints.
- Detect places that look close but are separated by water, terrain, rail, or entrance direction.
- Identify fixed activities that may anchor the route.

## 3. Evaluate value and friction together

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Name | English/translated name plus map-searchable local name |
| Travel value | One sentence on irreplaceability; no marketing copy |
| Fit | Connect to goals, days, travelers, budget, pace, transport, or hard constraints; say when unknown |
| Zone and access | Real arrival method from current route |
| Suggested time | Range and whether queue/internal movement are included |
| Time constraints | Opening, booking, last admission, season, or light |
| Convenience friction | Low/medium/high with transport, booking, effort, and return cost |
| Main cost | Ticket, detour, queue, effort, or weather risk |
| Information status | Verified / Recheck / Uncertain, shown only when it changes the choice |
| Photo note | Only when visual value exists |

Use reliable maps for coordinates; never guess. Give useful historical period and details rather than an encyclopaedia entry.

## 4. Grade by this trip

- `A Core`: high fit, hard to replace, worth structuring around.
- `B Recommended`: high value, selectable by geography, time, or current status.
- `C Conditional`: add when nearby, weather permits, at night, or with spare energy.
- `D Do not include`: marketing exceeds experience, cost is poor, conditions fail, or a much better substitute exists.

Show only famous, high-marketing-risk, or likely-to-be-questioned D items. Use `High marketing risk`, not an unsupported accusation. With insufficient trip data, separate general value from fit conditions still to confirm.

## 5. Conditional output

- Broad candidate request: boundary, spatial zones, A/B/C table, selected D exclusions, friction, booking/season, and key trade-offs.
- Direct itinerary request: use the master candidates internally and show only decision-relevant findings.
- Unknown duration: show route skeletons by duration, labelled not final.
- Coverage review: explain inclusion or rejection of important candidates.
