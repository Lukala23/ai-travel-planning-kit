# Travel health and medical planning rules

Read when expected sleeping or activity altitude is around 2,450–2,500 m or higher; vaccination/prophylaxis or entry-health evidence matters; the traveler carries controlled medicine, needles, cold-chain supplies, or a medical device; the user mentions a trip-relevant condition, chronic disease, severe allergy, pregnancy, or prior altitude response; remote medical/evacuation access changes feasibility; or the user explicitly asks for health planning.

For an ordinary city trip with only routine food/water, sun, mosquito, or mild climate exposure, give concise practical reminders in the route without loading a full health questionnaire or readiness card. This module plans and triages risk; it does not diagnose, replace a clinician, or invent individual prescriptions or doses.

## 1. Run a light health-risk screen first

Use the exact destination, transit points, dates, season, altitude, lodging environment, and activities rather than labelling a whole country uniformly. Screen at least:

- official entry-health requirements versus travel-health recommendations;
- vaccine-preventable illness, exposure that may require malaria or other prophylaxis assessment, and current outbreak/public-health events;
- mosquito/tick, food/water, heat, cold, sun, air quality, and animal-contact exposure;
- expected sleeping/activity altitude, ascent speed, remoteness, and evacuation time;
- medication carriage, medical access, language, payment, and travel-insurance coordination.

Do not produce a full medical chapter merely because a destination is tropical/subtropical; deepen only when risk, traveller conditions, or decision value triggers it. Conversely, non-hiking high-altitude cities, cruises, family visits, business, and ordinary sightseeing can trigger it.

## 2. Collect only decision-relevant health conditions

Ask only when needed:

- route, dates, duration, expected sleeping/activity altitude, and activity intensity;
- age band, pregnancy/trying to conceive, mobility or sensory assistance;
- chronic conditions that affect travel, severe allergy, previous altitude response, or recent material treatment;
- medication generic name, form, storage/cold-chain, needles/device needs, and approximate quantity;
- whether routine/travel vaccinations are complete or unknown;
- risks the user wants addressed and acceptable medical-access level.

Do not request identity numbers, a full medical record, prescription number, insurance account, or irrelevant diagnosis detail. Keep only necessary categories in deliverables and tell the user to store full medication, clinician, and emergency details securely themselves.

## 3. Verify pre-travel clinical and public-health needs

- Separate mandatory entry/boarding requirements from exposure-based health advice. A certificate may be a legal document or clinical evidence; do not mix authorities.
- Prefer current destination/residence public-health bodies, WHO, competent border/consular authorities, and qualified travel-medicine services. Outbreaks, vaccines, and prophylaxis are dynamic.
- When vaccination, prophylaxis, chronic-condition adjustment, pregnancy, altitude, or complex activity matters, recommend early review with a travel-medicine or treating clinician. Do not substitute one fixed lead time for the actual schedule, departure date, and person.
- Label clinical decisions `Professional assessment required`; never decide contraindication, interaction, prescription selection, or dosage.
- If departure is close, do not assume nothing can be done. Verify still-useful measures, route alternatives, and risk reduction, and advise prompt professional review.

## 4. Prescription/OTC medicine and medical devices

- Check controlled/psychotropic/narcotic medicine, injection, liquid, oxygen, cold-chain, and battery-device customs, airline, and security rules for every destination and transit point. A lawful home prescription does not prove import legality.
- Recommend reasonable travel quantity in original packaging plus generic name, purpose, and dosing record, with prescription, clinician letter, translation, legalisation, or prior permit only as the competent authority requires.
- Separate cabin and checked baggage. Essential medicine, rescue medicine, equipment, and documents generally need delay/lost-bag resilience, subject to liquid, needle, battery, and refrigeration rules.
- Verify time-zone, storage temperature, refrigerator reliability, power/plug, backup battery, consumables, and lawful replacement access. Never advise changing medication timing or dose without clinician confirmation.
- Use international generic names, not brand alone. Similar pharmacy names or packaging do not prove identical ingredient, strength, or authenticity.

## 5. Altitude is not only a hiking issue

- Apply this section to cities, cable cars, road/rail routes, flight arrivals, and hikes with expected sleeping/activity altitude around 2,450–2,500 m or higher. Judge sleeping altitude, ascent, prior acclimatisation, and individual history—not only the highest viewpoint.
- Fitness does not prevent altitude illness, and a previous uneventful trip does not guarantee this one. Material prior symptoms, cardiopulmonary disease, pregnancy, or related conditions require qualified review before irreversible booking.
- Prefer gradual ascent, lighter initial activity, sleep, and hydration space; avoid immediate strenuous activity, alcohol, or sedating plans after rapid arrival. Individual ascent/prophylaxis decisions must follow authoritative clinical guidance.
- State each high-altitude stage's sleeping altitude, maximum activity altitude, prior-night altitude, descent option, transport, and expected time to suitable care.
- Do not continue forced ascent when symptoms appear. Cite an authoritative symptom/action framework; breathing difficulty, altered consciousness, inability to walk steadily, fainting, or other severe/rapid deterioration requires immediate local emergency help or urgent descent, not diagnosis through chat.

## 6. Food/water, vector, and environmental exposure

- Verify authoritative current advice for tap water, ice, tooth brushing, raw food, dairy, and street food. Local use or hotel category is not proof of traveller safety.
- Where mosquito, tick, or vector risk is material, give executable skin coverage, repellent, net/air-conditioning, activity-time, and lodging controls, and identify any need for clinical prophylaxis assessment.
- For heat, sun, cold, dryness, pollution, or wildfire smoke, state exposure windows, clothing/hydration/rest, indoor fallback, and departure-day monitoring source.
- For diarrhoea, heat illness, sunburn, bites, motion sickness, and other common problems, give only authoritative prevention, self-care boundaries, and care triggers. Lower the care threshold for chronic illness, pregnancy, infants, older travellers, or severe/persistent symptoms.
- Do not recommend medicine of unclear origin, prescription, or authenticity merely because it is locally available.

## 7. Destination care pathway

Verify in proportion to trip risk:

- emergency number and consular/official medical-assistance entry point;
- appropriate clinic/hospital tier, 24-hour capability, language, and travel time;
- advance payment/direct billing, deposit, card/cash, insurer authorisation, and receipt/record access;
- lawful pharmacies, prescription rules, weekend/night coverage;
- remote rescue, referral, ground/air evacuation, and weather limitations.

“A hospital is nearby” does not prove trauma, altitude, paediatric, maternity, or intensive-care capability. For remote/high-risk routes, give a primary care point, escalation facility, and evacuation direction, and reconcile them with `travel-insurance.md` territory, activity, and evacuation wording.

## 8. Chronic conditions, accessibility, and companions

- Allow medication and delay reserve, rest, meals, storage, charging, and review/testing windows; a treating clinician confirms all individual changes.
- Verify airline/operator rules for devices, mobility aids, oxygen, refrigeration, assistance, and evidence; airport generic guidance alone is insufficient.
- Companions need only the emergency action, medication/device location, and help channel the traveller chooses to share. Keep full health privacy out of a public guide.
- For solo, language-constrained, or remote travel, add check-ins, translation card, offline medical summary, and missed-check-in escalation.

## 9. Deliver a health-readiness card only when useful

After the module triggers, select only items relevant to the current risk. Provide a readiness card when the risk materially changes preparation, routing, or help-seeking; do not complete it for template symmetry:

1. `Low identified health risk / Manageable with preparation / Professional assessment required before decision / Currently not advised / Cannot yet assess`;
2. trigger, applicable route/date, and evidence date;
3. clinical, vaccine/prophylaxis open items and latest useful action point;
4. lawful carriage, storage, backup, and evidence for medicine/devices;
5. trip-specific altitude, food/water, vector, climate/air-quality actions;
6. suitable care point, emergency number, payment/insurance process, and evacuation escalation;
7. only relevant checks among `Now / T-30 / T-7 / T-48 / Same day`.

State source and limitation for clinical claims. If traveller conditions or authoritative current information are missing, keep the uncertainty visible and do not create a safety guarantee with generic prose.
