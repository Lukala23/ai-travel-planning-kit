# International Payments, Connectivity, and Local Operations

Read only when the current request needs payment, exchange, connectivity, billing/tipping, legal-cultural, or practical operating guidance abroad, or when an executable international route materially depends on it. Do not load this module for a narrow visa or transit answer.

`entry-and-transit.md` owns border legality; `public-transit.md` owns operator-specific ticketing. This file owns how the traveler can operate reliably after arrival.

## 1. Choose payment by actual coverage and resilience

Cash, physical cards, and digital wallets are all candidates. Start with payment methods the traveler already has and can reuse in the relevant regions, then compare them on exact acceptance, complete cost, setup burden, network/battery dependency, and failure consequence. Do not assume a home country, card network, wallet, or preferred currency.

The presence of a wallet, contactless mark, QR scheme, or card-network logo does not prove that the traveler's account works. Verify only layers that affect the decision:

- traveler side: device/OS, NFC, region, app, linked card or balance, and cross-border eligibility;
- acceptance side: exact merchant/operator, product, and payment scenario;
- settlement: currency, exchange, fees, pre-authorisation, limits, and refund;
- trip operation: activation, data dependency, multiple travelers, and recovery after failure.

Recommend a destination-only wallet or app only when it is unusually useful for a key activity, or lacking it causes material inconvenience, price loss, or loss of access, and the gain outweighs registration, identity, local-number, funding, language, refund, and residual-balance burden. Do not create an account merely for template completeness.

## 2. Cash, exchange, and independent fallback

- Identify cash-only or cash-preferred situations, then estimate first-arrival, daily, remote, and emergency cash. Do not exchange a large amount merely because the trip is international.
- Compare realistic access and complete cost across home-bank exchange, airports, licensed local exchange, banks, and reliable ATMs: spread, fixed fee, ATM/operator and issuer fees, limits, and leftover-cash loss.
- Identify dynamic currency conversion separately. Compare local-currency settlement with the traveler's card-currency option using actual issuer terms; do not let a terminal default decide silently.
- Primary and backup must not depend on one phone, card, or account. Keep an independent physical fallback and state the response to dead battery, fraud controls, SMS failure, decline, or retained card.
- Recommend only licensed exchange and reliable ATMs; flag street exchange, counterfeit, skimming, and cash-storage risk.

## 3. Connectivity

Compare options available from the traveler's home market and device: current-carrier roaming, manufacturer service, travel eSIM, destination SIM/eSIM, and pocket Wi-Fi when group use warrants it.

Verify only choice-changing facts:

- exact device model and sales region, bands, lock status, dual-SIM/eSIM support;
- route coverage, network, throttling/fair-use, hotspot, start point, validity, renewal, and tax;
- local number, calls/SMS, and required verification-message support—a data-only service has no local SMS unless explicitly provided;
- local identity rules, official sales channel, activation, renewal/closure, and local-payment requirements without requesting identity numbers;
- pre-installation, arrival activation, APN/data-roaming settings, support, and a second contact path after loss, expiry, no signal, or no power.

Use existing roaming when coverage, price, and allowance are adequate; use eSIM only when the exact device supports it; choose a local SIM when a local number, better coverage, longer stay, or materially lower complete cost justifies it. Save maps, lodging addresses, tickets, routes, and emergency contacts offline regardless of the primary option.

## 4. Taxes, tips, and bills

Research only settings the traveler will use and separate:

- statutory VAT/sales tax;
- mandatory service, city/tourist/accommodation/resort fees;
- cover, bread/water, minimum-spend, cleaning, or platform fees;
- card, currency, ATM, or booking fees;
- refundable deposit or pre-authorisation;
- voluntary tip.

State name, amount/range, mandatory versus optional status, usual inclusion in displayed prices, collector, payment, and receipt. Distinguish law, industry norm, and recent traveler experience. Do not generalize one country, city, or business to its neighbors.

For an unexplained charge: request an itemised bill, identify the line and whether it is mandatory, compare the menu or booking terms, verify terminal amount/currency, ask a manager or platform when needed, and preserve evidence. Avoid accidental double tipping after a service charge. Do not prescribe confrontation; personal and document safety comes first.

## 5. Law, culture, and practical operation

Cover only facts that change behavior or routing now:

- weekends, religious days, midday closures, strikes, festivals, and booking norms;
- identity carry, lodging registration, named tickets, and security checks;
- dress, religion, alcohol, public conduct, photography, and gender-related rules;
- legitimate taxi ranks, licensed services, common scams, surge/extra charges, and change; hand consequential or negotiated transport to `taxi-charter.md`;
- relevant voltage/plugs, languages, emergency number, and consular entry point.

Credible recent sources may support low-consequence local practice as planning judgment. Verify law, mandatory charges, and safety under the high-consequence standard.

## 6. Output boundary

Output only the payment, exchange, connectivity, or local-operating guidance needed now. For a complete international route, a compact card may use `Scenario | Primary | Backup | Cost/condition | Failure action | Source/recheck`. Do not force a safety card, exchange chapter, connectivity chapter, or large checklist into a narrow answer.
