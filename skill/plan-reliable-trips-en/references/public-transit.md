# Public transport, fares, and payment

Read for bus, metro, rail, flight, ferry, or public shuttle.

## 1. Verify the actual connection

- Use the operator's current station names, timetable, rules, works, and suspension.
- Determine whether stations/platforms/terminals/piers are co-located and how long entrances take.
- Check exit/re-entry, security, border control, baggage reclaim, new ticket, or second payment during transfer.
- Verify first/last service, frequency, reservation, luggage, and the next feasible option after a miss.

## 2. Calculate door-to-door time

Include station access, ticket/top-up, wait, transfer walk, platform finding, destination entrance, and buffer. A 30-minute train is not a 30-minute movement.

For must-catch flights, trains, or ferries, give a conservative arrival window with reasons. Minimum connection is not a guarantee.

## 3. Understand fare before choosing payment

For each operator, verify:

- single, distance/zone fare, cap, pass, reservation, and seat ticket relationships;
- whether the same card, device, or token must tap in and out;
- whether transfer discounts or caps cross operators, and child/luggage tickets;
- payment methods on vehicle, at station, in app/site, or convenience store;
- action for low balance, failed payment, dead phone, or no data; whether e-tickets work offline.

## 4. Payment priority

When actually compatible:

1. an already configured cross-border wallet or open-loop contactless method with no new account;
2. the user's existing international contactless physical card;
3. an anonymous/visitor transit card without local phone, ID, or bank account;
4. cash/paper ticket as normal or emergency fallback;
5. a local app/wallet only when material time/cost benefit outweighs registration, identity, top-up, language, and balance exit.

The presence of Alipay, WeChat Pay, Apple Pay, Google Wallet, UnionPay, Visa, or Mastercard branding does not prove the user's account works. Verify:

- **user side**: OS, NFC, region, app version, linked card/balance and international/transport eligibility;
- **acceptance side**: exact operator, gate, line, ticket, and scenario;
- **settlement**: currency, fee, exchange, pre-authorisation, offline transaction, limits, refund;
- **trip operation**: activation, data dependency, multiple travelers, transfer, and cap preservation.

## 5. Independent fallback

- Primary and backup must not share one phone, card, or account.
- Carry at least one physical method. Decide cash role, currency, and amount from local acceptance, exchange convenience, and full cost; do not exchange a large amount mechanically.
- State actions for dead battery, fraud controls, SMS failure, declined card, and low transit balance.
- Do not mix a phone wallet and its underlying physical card for tap-in/out; they may be separate tokens.
- When tickets, navigation, or ride-hail depend on the phone, also apply connectivity rules. Transit card and SIM/eSIM are different products.

## 6. Evaluate convenience for this trip

- Verify lifts and walking links for large luggage, stroller, children, older travelers, or accessibility.
- At night, verify last service and safe connection; adjust the route if return is unstable.
- Compare passes and cards only against this trip.
- Before a transit card, verify purchase point, issue/deposit, initial value, top-up, balance, refund, validity, residual balance, one-card-per-person, and child rules.
- Do not buy a card when existing phone/card gets the same transfer or cap.
- Use local searchable station, line, exit, and purchase names.

Deliver a short table: segment, fare product, primary payment, setup, backup, and failure action.
